2023

  1. New results and bounds on codes over GF (17)
    Eric Chen, Nuh Aydin, Fredrik Jönsson, and 1 more author
    Journal of Algebra Combinatorics Discrete Structures and Applications, Oct 2023
    Abs DOI

    Determining the best possible values of the parameters of a linear code is one of the most fundamental and challenging problems in coding theory. There exist databases of best-known linear codes (BKLC) over small finite fields. In this work, we establish a database of BKLCs over the field GF (17) together with upper bounds on the minimum distances for lengths up to 150 and dimensions up to 6. In the process, we have found many new linear codes over GF (17).

  2. New good quasi-cyclic codes and codes with improved minimum distance
    Eric Chen and Fredrik Jönsson
    Journal of Algebra Combinatorics Discrete Structures and Applications, Jan 2023
    Abs

    One of the most important and challenging problems in coding theory is to construct codes with optimal parameters. As a generalization of cyclic codes, quasi-cyclic (QC) codes as well as quasi-twisted (QT) codes have been shown to contain record-breaking codes. In this paper, various computer algorithms have been used to search for good QC codes. A lot of good new QC codes have been found and they have been used to construct new linear codes. A total 11 new codes that improve the bound on the minimum distance are presented.

  1. Theoretical analysis of a correlation attack based on convolutional codes
    Thomas Johansson and Fredrik Jönsson
    IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, Aug 2002
    Funding Information: Manuscript received November 5, 2001; revised April 2, 2002. This work was supported by the Foundation for Strategic Research–PCC under Grant 9706-09. The material in this paper was presented in part at the IEEE International Symposium on Information Theory, Sorrento, Italy, 2000. The authors are with the Department of Information Technology, Lund University, 221 00 Lund, Sweden (e-mail: thomas@it.lth.se; fredrikj@it.lth.se). Communicated by N. I. Koblitz, Associate Editor for Complexity and Cryptography. Publisher Item Identifier 10.1109/TIT.2002.800494.
    Abs DOI

    One general class of attacks on stream ciphers is correlation attacks. Most of previous results regarding performance of correlation attacks have been based entirely on simulations. In this paper, we use random coding bounds for convolutional codes to give a theoretical analysis of a previously proposed correlation attack based on convolutional codes. The results from the theoretical derivation are verified by simulations.

  2. On the complexity of some cryptographic problems based on the general decoding problem
    Thomas Johansson and Fredrik Jönsson
    IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, Oct 2002
    Funding Information: Manuscript received February 8, 2001; revised May 24, 2001. This work was supported by the Foundation for Strategic Research–PCC under Grant 9706-09. The material in this paper was presented in part at the IEEE International Symposium on Information Theory, MIT, Cambridge, MA, August 1998.
    Abs DOI

    A new probabilistic algorithm for decoding one received word from a set of many given received words, into a codeword such that the Hamming distance between the received word and the codeword is at most t, is proposed. The new algorithm is applicable to several cryptographic problems, such as the Stern identification scheme, the McEliece public-key cryptosystem, and in correlation attacks on stream ciphers. When applicable, it runs significantly faster than previous algorithms used for attacks on these cryptosystems.

  3. A fast correlation attack on LILI-128
    Fredrik Jönsson and Thomas Johansson
    Information Processing Letters, Feb 2002
    Abs DOI

    In this paper we demonstrate a fast correlation attack on the recently proposed stream cipher LILI-128. The attack has complexity around 271 bit operations assuming a received sequence of length around 230 bits and a precomputation phase of complexity 279 table lookups. This complexity is significantly lower than 2112, which was conjectured by the inventors of LILI-128 to be a lower bound on the complexity of any attack.

  1. Theoretical analysis of a correlation attack based on convolutional codes
    F. Jönsson and T. Johansson
    Feb 2000
    2000 IEEE International Symposium on Information Theory ; Conference date: 25-06-2000 Through 30-06-2000
    Abs

    Consider a binary synchronous stream cipher where a correlation has been identified between the keystream sequence and the output from one of the LFSRs. Hence, a correlation attack can be applied. By using an estimate from the Viterbi algorithm, the corresponding estimate of the initial state of the LFSR is derived.

  2. Fast correlation attacks through reconstruction of linear polynomials
    Thomas Johansson and Fredrik Jönsson
    Feb 2000
    Publisher Copyright: \textcopyright Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 2000.; 20th Annual International Cryptology Conference, CRYPTO 2000 ; Conference date: 20-08-2000 Through 24-08-2000
    Abs DOI

    The task of a fast correlation attack is to efficiently restore the initial content of a linear feedback shift register in a stream cipher using a detected correlation with the output sequence. We show that by modeling this problem as the problem of learning a binary linear multivariate polynomial, algorithms for polynomial reconstruction with queries can be modified through some general techniques used in fast correlation attacks. The result is a new and efficient way of performing fast correlation attacks.

  1. Improved fast correlation attacks on stream ciphers via convolutional codes
    Thomas Johansson and Fredrik Jönsson
    Feb 1999
    Publisher Copyright: \textcopyright Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 1999.; 7th International Conference on the Theory and Application of Cryptographic Techniques, EUROCRYPT 1999 ; Conference date: 02-05-1999 Through 06-05-1999
    Abs DOI

    This paper describes new methods for fast correlation attacks, based on the theory of convolutional codes. They can be applied to arbitrary LFSR feedback polynomials, in opposite to the previous methods, which mainly focus on feedback polynomials of low weight. The results improve signifficantly the few previous results for this general case, and are in many cases comparable with corresponding results for low weight feedback polynomials.

  2. Fast correlation attacks based on turbo code techniques
    Thomas Johansson and Fredrik Jönsson
    Feb 1999
    Publisher Copyright: \textcopyright Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 1999.; 19th Annual International Cryptology Conference, CRYPTO 1999 ; Conference date: 15-08-1999 Through 19-08-1999
    Abs DOI

    This paper describes new methods for fast correlation attacks on stream ciphers, based on techniques used for constructing and decoding the by now famous turbo codes. The proposed algorithm consists of two parts, a preprocessing part and a decoding part. The preprocessing part identifies several parallel convolutional codes, embedded in the code generated by the LFSR, all sharing the same information bits. The decoding part then finds the correct information bits through an iterative decoding procedure. This provides the initial state of the LFSR.

  3. Correlation attacks, convolutional codes, and iterative decoding
    Thomas Johansson and Fredrik Jönsson
    Feb 1999
    Publisher Copyright: \textcopyright 1999 IEEE.; 1999 IEEE Information Theory and Communications Workshop ; Conference date: 25-06-1999
    Abs DOI

    This paper overviews new methods for fast correlation attacks on stream ciphers, based on convolutional codes. Identification of embedded structures of concatenated convolutional codes (both parallel and serial) in the shift register code, providing increased performance, is considered.
